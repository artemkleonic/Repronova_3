Fertility preservation and future-use decisions

When reproductive material is stored for future treatment, the documents need to address more than the initial procedure. We help individuals and prospective parents understand the arrangements governing storage and identify future decisions that need clearer instructions.

Reviewing the arrangements now

We examine storage agreements, consent forms, payment and renewal provisions and the process for maintaining contact with the provider. The review can identify restrictions on future use and questions about what happens when a notice is unanswered, storage ends or another clinic is to receive the material.

Changes in personal circumstances

Separation, a move abroad or a change in the people whose consent is required can raise questions about future treatment. We assess the documents and identify matters that need clarification under the relevant rules. The fact that material remains in storage does not determine whether a particular proposed use will be authorised.

Incapacity and posthumous use

Where a person can no longer make decisions or has died, the evidence of consent and the proposed use require a specific assessment. We can review the available instructions and the provider’s position, explain the questions requiring specialist input and prepare a written legal opinion within an agreed scope.

You can seek advice when preparing documents or when circumstances have already changed. Start with a brief description of the material, providers and countries involved, and the decision you need to address. Sensitive records can be requested through an agreed channel later.
