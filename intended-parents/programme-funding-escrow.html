Surrogacy programme funding and escrow

The funding model of a surrogacy programme determines when parents commit money, who holds it and how it becomes available to the providers. We advise on the legal and contractual structure of programme funding, including proposed escrow arrangements and alternatives based on staged or direct payments.

Comparing the proposed arrangements

A substantial advance payment, payments linked to milestones and third-party management of funds involve different contractual responsibilities. We help compare the terms available for the particular programme, including payment timing, authority over the funds and the evidence required before money is released. Availability and suitability need to be checked for the countries and providers involved.

The recipient and the payment basis

We examine which entity receives each payment, the basis for receiving it and how the programme budget is reflected in the agreements. The review can address additional treatment cycles, changed arrangements, unplanned costs and who must authorise expenditure beyond the agreed amount.

Escrow and release conditions

If an escrow or other funds-management arrangement is proposed, we review its actual terms. Questions include who holds the money, who can authorise release, which evidence is required and what happens when instructions are disputed or a programme stops. The label alone does not establish the protection provided.

Changes and refunds

We examine how unused balances, deductions, termination and refunds are addressed and who is responsible for accounting to parents. The outcome may be a written assessment of the proposed funding structure, questions for the agency or escrow provider and proposed contractual changes.

We advise on the arrangement without holding the programme funds ourselves or guaranteeing repayment. For the initial enquiry, describe the proposed programme, the countries involved and the payment or escrow model you have been offered.
