Why REPRONOVA

How we work, whose interests we represent and what clients can expect from an engagement.

Independence

We do not sell clinic packages and do not accept hidden referral or commission fees.

Choosing a clinic or agency can involve advice from several organisations with different commercial interests. Our engagement identifies whose interests we advise on and the work we are asked to undertake. We examine proposed arrangements from that client’s perspective, including questions that may need to be raised with the provider. Any third-party payment arrangement must be disclosed and must not compromise the independence of our advice.

Legal clarity

We assess legal structure, contracts, parentage, citizenship and documentation risks before clients commit to a programme.

A programme may be described as available in one country while leaving questions unresolved in another. We examine how the proposed route fits the client’s circumstances and where contracts, consents or documents require further attention. Our advice distinguishes established requirements from assumptions, unresolved issues and matters requiring local confirmation. This helps clients understand what can be assessed before committing and which outcomes depend on later decisions or procedures.

International reach

We work through legal research and coordination with local counsel in relevant jurisdictions.

Our work considers the countries connected with the people, treatment, reproductive material and intended legal outcome. Sergii Antonov’s practical experience includes assisting with reproductive programmes in Ukraine, Georgia and Mexico. Where local advice or representation is needed, we coordinate with appropriately qualified professionals on an agreed basis. The relevant responsibilities and any separate appointments are clarified for the client, so that advice from different jurisdictions addresses the same factual situation.

Understanding the medical context

Our legal analysis takes account of how assisted reproduction works in practice, including treatment stages, consent, laboratory processes and the handling of reproductive material.

Legal questions in assisted reproduction often turn on what happened during treatment, how consent was recorded or how material and information were handled. Sergii Antonov’s education in law, medicine and forensic examination informs the identification of relevant facts and documents. This helps us formulate precise questions for clinics and specialists. Where a matter requires a clinical assessment or expert evidence, that work is undertaken by an appropriate specialist within a separately defined role.

Practical advice for decisions

We explain what the findings mean for your next step: questions to raise, terms to reconsider, documents to obtain and issues that require further assessment.

A legal opinion should help the client decide what to do with the information. Depending on the agreed task, our work may identify amendments to a contract, questions for a provider, missing evidence or alternative approaches requiring further assessment. We explain the reasons for our conclusions and the limits of the material reviewed. For professional clients, this may also involve translating legal requirements into usable documents and procedures for their organisation.

Your interests represented

Under an agreed mandate, we act as your independent legal adviser in correspondence and discussions with agencies, clinics and other participants, addressing obligations and unresolved issues.

Clients sometimes need someone to follow up on a question, request documents or challenge an unclear response throughout a programme. Within agreed authority, we can conduct correspondence, seek explanations about contractual obligations and help address difficulties with agencies, clinics or other participants. This is particularly relevant to intended parents seeking independent legal representation during a surrogacy programme. Before accepting an instruction, we identify the client, check for conflicts and establish the limits of our authority.

Clear scope and fees

The scope of work, deliverables and fees are agreed before an engagement begins. Additional tasks and external professional costs are addressed separately, so clients understand what is included.

A consultation, document review and ongoing representation involve different responsibilities. We define the assignment and the expected output, whether that is advice in a meeting, a written opinion, revised documents or specified ongoing assistance. Work outside the agreed scope is discussed before it is undertaken. For clinics, agencies and gamete banks, annual support can be arranged as comprehensive prepaid legal assistance over twelve months, covering an agreed list and volume of work.

Support as matters develop

Support can begin with a single question and extend to document preparation, programme communications or a later difficulty. Each stage is agreed around the work the client needs.

A matter may change after an initial review: a provider may propose new terms, a programme may be interrupted or a document may need to be used in another country. Clients can request further assistance at those stages. Where we continue an existing matter, we use the relevant background already established and identify what needs to be updated. Further work remains subject to an agreed scope and any additional expertise or local involvement required.

Experience in resolving disputes

Experience in reproductive medicine disputes informs our approach to complaints, compensation issues and negotiations. We assess the facts, the client’s objectives and the available routes to resolution.

Sergii Antonov has helped resolve dozens of conflicts in reproductive medicine, including disputes concerning embryo transfer errors, treatment quality, surrogacy programme management and the loss of embryos or gametes. This experience helps identify the records, expert input and practical issues that may matter in a disagreement. Depending on the circumstances, assistance may involve a complaint, negotiations or preparation for further legal action. Neutral mediation and facilitation require a separate role and mandate from representing one party.
