Parentage, citizenship and travel documentation

Following assisted reproduction abroad, families may need to address several distinct legal questions: who is recognised as a parent, how the birth is recorded, whether nationality is acquired or must be applied for, and which documents permit travel. We help clients identify the steps relevant to their circumstances.

Reviewing the proposed route

We consider the parents’ nationalities and residence, the countries of treatment and birth, and the proposed programme documents. The purpose is to establish which authorities and professional advisers need to be involved and what evidence they may require.

Preparing the documentary plan

An assignment may include reviewing the documents expected from the clinic and other providers, identifying questions for the registry or consular authority and preparing a sequence of actions. Translation, certification, court procedures and applications should be considered according to the actual route and are included only where expressly agreed and deliverable.

When a difficulty has already arisen

If a document has been refused or further evidence requested, we examine the written response and supporting material. We help define the issue and the next enquiry or professional instruction. The requirements of the receiving authority must be distinguished from the provider’s own description of its process.

Scope and timing

We do not promise a passport, nationality, recognition decision or fixed travel date. The work supports a properly prepared approach to the relevant procedures; decisions remain with the competent authorities.

To begin, state the countries, the child’s expected or actual place of birth, the family’s intended destination and any document already issued or requested.
