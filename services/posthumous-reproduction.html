Posthumous reproduction and future-use decisions

The proposed use of reproductive material after a person’s death, or when they can no longer make decisions, raises questions that require careful examination of the facts, consent evidence and applicable legal framework. We provide a defined legal assessment for individuals and organisations facing these circumstances.

Establishing the documentary position

We review the available consent forms, storage and treatment agreements, relevant instructions and correspondence from the provider. The assessment identifies what the documents establish, where their wording is uncertain and what further evidence or professional input is required.

Connecting the relevant issues

The ability to store or release material, authorisation for treatment, recognition of parentage and any succession-related questions may require different analyses. We identify the jurisdictions and decision-makers relevant to the proposed use rather than assuming that a single document resolves the whole matter.

Planning and existing difficulties

Advice can be sought in advance, when recording future-use instructions, or after an event has occurred. Where a clinic has requested clarification or declined to act, we examine its stated reasons and help formulate the questions to be addressed. Urgent enquiries are assessed according to their facts and the available capacity.

The result of the assignment

The deliverable may be a written legal opinion, comments on documentation or a plan for obtaining specialist advice and approaching the relevant provider or authority. We do not assume that treatment or release of material will be permitted.
