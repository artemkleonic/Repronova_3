Frequently Asked Questions

Practical answers about our services and the legal questions that arise in assisted reproduction. These materials provide general information and questions for discussion. An individual legal assessment depends on the facts, documents and jurisdictions involved.

Working with REPRONOVA

What does REPRONOVA do?

We provide legal and strategic advice on assisted reproduction, including programme review, contracts, written legal opinions, independent representation and support for professional organisations. The scope is agreed for the particular matter.

Do you organise programmes or recruit surrogates and donors?

REPRONOVA does not recruit or match surrogates or donors. We can assist with identifying relevant professional organisations, reviewing proposed arrangements and coordinating legal advice.

Can you advise before I choose a country or agency?

Yes. An initial assessment can identify the jurisdictions and legal questions that should inform your decision. It may consider the proposed programme, your residence and citizenship, the intended parentage arrangements and the route for the child’s documentation.

Can you work with a programme that has already started?

Yes, subject to an initial review and agreement on scope. We can examine the documents and current position, communicate with providers under an agreed mandate and help address unresolved issues.

How are fees and the work agreed?

We discuss the proposed scope, output and fees before work begins. A consultation, a written opinion and ongoing representation are different services. Any third-party professional costs should be identified and agreed separately.

Can consultations take place remotely?

Remote consultations and document work can be arranged. Availability and the need for local counsel depend on the matter and relevant jurisdictions. Tell us your preferred communication language and any deadline.

Programmes documents and payments

Can you independently represent intended parents?

Yes. An agreed mandate can cover correspondence with an agency or clinic, document requests, review of obligations, programme changes and problem resolution. Formal authority and local representation requirements are considered for the task.

Is an agency review the same as a programme review?

They overlap but address different questions. An agency review concerns the organisation and available evidence about its operations. A programme review examines the proposed contractual structure, participants, payments and legal steps for your circumstances.

Does an escrow account guarantee that my money is safe?

No. The account provider, legal structure, release conditions and treatment of disputes or insolvency all matter. We can review the proposed arrangement and identify questions to resolve before funding it. An escrow arrangement does not replace insurance or programme due diligence.

Will a birth certificate be sufficient for travel and parentage recognition?

That cannot be assumed. Parentage, citizenship, passport issuance and permission to enter another country may require separate steps. A plan should address the countries of birth and intended residence, together with the parents’ citizenships.

Can you review donor agreements and storage documents?

Yes. A review may address consent, permitted uses, information rights, future instructions, storage and transfer responsibilities. The scope depends on the documents and the countries concerned.

Can reproductive material be used after someone dies?

The answer depends on the applicable law, valid instructions and the proposed treatment. Retrieval, storage, use and the child’s legal status are distinct questions. This requires an individual assessment rather than reliance on a general contract clause.

Disputes professional clients and enquiries

Do you assist surrogates and donors?

Yes. We can provide independent advice on agreements, consent, rights, compensation terms and disputes. We first establish whose interests we would represent and assess any conflict of interest.

Can you help with compensation following complications or death?

We can assess contractual, insurance and potential liability issues and coordinate appropriate specialist advice. The position may involve the affected person or, following a death, those entitled to bring a claim. Entitlement and evidence require case-specific assessment; payment is not guaranteed.

Can mediation be used when a programme goes wrong?

Mediation may be appropriate where the parties are willing to explore a negotiated resolution. Its suitability depends on the dispute. Urgent protective steps, medical care and legal deadlines should be considered separately.

Do clinics agencies and banks have an annual support option?

Yes. Comprehensive prepaid legal support can be agreed for one year against a defined list of services. The agreement should specify deliverables, any workload limits, communication arrangements and work charged separately.

What should I send with the first enquiry?

Provide your contact details, the countries involved, a brief description and any relevant deadline. Please do not send medical records, genetic data, identity documents or identifying details about other participants through the initial form. We will agree document sharing if needed.

Does submitting a request create a professional engagement?

No. We first review whether we can assist, consider conflicts and agree the scope and terms. A submitted enquiry does not confirm that a deadline is being monitored or that representation has begun.
