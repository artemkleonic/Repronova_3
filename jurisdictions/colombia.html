Colombia Surrogacy and Assisted Reproduction Legal Guide

For a programme involving Colombia, the first task is to establish the legal basis of the particular arrangement and the procedure for recognising the intended parents. A provider’s willingness to accept a client does not answer these questions.

Legislation and court decisions

Colombian constitutional case law is relevant to surrogacy-related questions. In judgment T-275 of 2022, the Constitutional Court addressed a parental-leave case involving surrogacy and called for legislative action. That judgment should not be presented as a comprehensive statutory scheme or general approval of every proposed programme. Current legislation and the decisions relevant to the intended arrangement need to be assessed together. [1]

Parentage and the child’s documents

Before treatment, establish who will appear in the initial birth record and how each intended parent’s legal status will be determined. Ask whether a further administrative or court procedure is required, which evidence supports it and what role the surrogate has in that process. The intended parents’ nationality, relationship status and genetic connection should be considered alongside the requirements of the country where the child will live.

The contractual and medical arrangements

Review who is responsible for clinical treatment, programme coordination, payments and post-birth documentation. The contract should address informed consent, medical decision-making, compensation, complications and the consequences of programme interruption. Check how reproductive material is sourced and documented, including donor consent and the availability of relevant records. Any proposal to alter, conceal or misstate the circumstances of birth requires immediate legal attention.

How REPRONOVA can help

We can review a proposed Colombian programme and identify the legal assumptions on which it depends. Our assistance can include contract review, questions for local counsel, an independent second legal opinion and representation in communications with an agency or clinic. We can also help coordinate the parentage and documentation questions that need to be resolved in Colombia and in the intended parents’ home jurisdiction before commitments are made.
