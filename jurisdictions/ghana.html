Ghana Surrogacy and Assisted Reproduction Legal Guide

For a Ghanaian surrogacy programme, legal planning should begin with the procedure for establishing parentage and registering the child. Eligibility, contractual responsibilities and the requirements of the intended parents’ home country need to be examined alongside that procedure.

The legal framework

Ghana’s Registration of Births and Deaths Act, 2020, Act 1027, contains a specific section on the registration of assisted reproductive births. Section 22 is therefore an important starting point for reviewing the proposed parentage and registration process. Its existence should not be treated as confirmation that every advertised programme or foreign family arrangement is legally suitable. [1]

Parentage and individual circumstances

Ask local counsel to identify the appropriate court and registration procedures, the evidence required and the timing of any application. The review should consider the intended parents’ nationality, relationship status and genetic connection. Same-sex clients also need advice on the implications of Ghana’s criminal law and the personal-safety context; a provider’s acceptance is not an assessment of those issues. [2]

Programme safeguards and documents

The clinic, agency and surrogate arrangements should explain medical consent, compensation, pregnancy and complication costs, and the records needed after delivery. Establish who is responsible for obtaining each document and what happens if an application is delayed or refused. Review payment stages, the handling of client funds and cancellation terms. The child’s birth registration, nationality, passport or other travel document, and recognition of parentage abroad should each have a defined legal route.

How REPRONOVA can help

We can review the proposed programme and its legal explanation, identify questions for Ghanaian counsel and assess the contracts and funding structure. Support can also include independent representation of intended parents in correspondence with providers and coordination of the documentation questions arising in the family’s home country. Where the proposed route depends on an unresolved legal issue, we explain what needs to be established before the client can make an informed commitment.

Legal sources

2 UK FCDO — Ghana safety and security, LGBT travellers
