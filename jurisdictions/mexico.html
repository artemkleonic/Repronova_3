Mexico Surrogacy and Assisted Reproduction Legal Guide

Legal planning for surrogacy in Mexico begins with the particular state and the proposed programme structure. The location of treatment, the surrogate’s residence and the intended place of birth all need to be identified before contracts are signed.

The legal framework

Mexico should not be treated as a single, uniform surrogacy jurisdiction. State family law, constitutional principles and judicial decisions can affect the proposed route. The Supreme Court’s 2021 review of Tabasco’s rules addressed restrictions on access and the surrogate’s autonomy. It does not remove the need to establish the procedure applicable to an individual arrangement. [1]

Eligibility and legal parentage

An assessment should consider the intended parents’ nationality, residence, relationship status and genetic connection with the child. Ask who will be recorded as the parents, whether a court application is needed and which authority will issue the birth record. A provider’s previous case is useful background, but the legal basis must fit your own circumstances. Recognition of parentage and the child’s nationality in your home country require a separate assessment.

Contracts and programme funding

The agency’s package, clinic agreement and surrogacy agreement should describe consistent responsibilities. Review treatment costs, surrogate payments, pregnancy complications, neonatal care, failed attempts and programme cancellation. Where an escrow or other managed account is proposed, establish who holds the funds, who authorises releases and what happens to unused money. Independent legal advice for the surrogate and respect for her medical decision-making should be addressed from the outset.

How REPRONOVA can help

We can review the proposed state-specific route, agency documentation, contracts and funding arrangements, and coordinate questions requiring Mexican legal advice. Under an agreed mandate, we can also represent intended parents in correspondence with agencies and clinics, follow up on contractual obligations and help address delays or disputed charges. The scope can include planning the documents needed after birth and identifying issues for counsel in the parents’ home country.
