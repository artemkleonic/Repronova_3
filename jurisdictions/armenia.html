Armenia Surrogacy and Assisted Reproduction Legal Guide

Eligibility is a central issue when considering surrogacy in Armenia. The intended parents’ circumstances and the surrogate’s citizenship can affect whether a proposed arrangement falls within the permitted framework.

The legal framework

Armenia’s Law on Human Reproductive Health and Reproductive Rights contains specific rules on assisted reproduction and surrogacy. Article 12 restricts the use of an Armenian-citizen surrogate by foreign citizens who are not of Armenian origin. This is a specific restriction, rather than a statement that all arrangements involving foreigners have the same status. The law also addresses medical grounds and restrictions affecting a single man without a genetic connection to the child. [1]

Assessing the proposed arrangement

A review should identify the nationality and relevant status of every participant, the intended parents’ medical circumstances and the source of the reproductive material. Any suggestion that a different nationality, residence arrangement or surrogate will resolve an eligibility issue requires a documented legal explanation. Ask which evidence and permissions are required and how parentage will be registered under that particular route.

Agreements and financial responsibility

Article 15 provides for a written, notarised surrogacy agreement and addresses compensation and responsibility for specified pregnancy, birth and complication-related expenses. [1] The practical review should examine the agreement alongside the clinic and agency documents: payment stages, medical costs, consents, record access and the procedure following delivery. The arrangements for DNA evidence and the documents needed for the child’s registration should be established before treatment.

How REPRONOVA can help

We can assess the eligibility questions raised by a proposed Armenian programme, review the contractual structure and identify the evidence needed for further local confirmation. For intended parents already dealing with a provider, we can assist with independent correspondence, requests for a written explanation of the legal route and review of changes to the programme. Advice can also address recognition of parentage and the documents required in the parents’ country of nationality or residence.

Legal sources
