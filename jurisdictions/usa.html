United States Surrogacy and Assisted Reproduction Legal Guide

A US surrogacy programme should be assessed at state level, with separate attention to the intended parents’ home-country requirements. The relevant law may depend on where the surrogate lives, where treatment takes place and where the child is expected to be born.

State law and the parentage process

Requirements differ between states, so a statement that surrogacy is available in the United States is not a complete legal assessment. California provides one example: Family Code section 7962 requires separate independent legal representation for the intended parent or parents and the gestational carrier before the agreement is executed. It also specifies agreement formalities and timing before preparatory medication or embryo transfer, and addresses parentage proceedings. These California provisions should not be applied automatically to another state. [1]

Funding and insurance

Review the programme budget alongside the surrogacy agreement, escrow terms and insurance arrangements. Identify who holds the funds, the conditions for each disbursement and the procedure for disputed or unused balances. Pregnancy care, complications, the surrogate’s cover and the newborn’s medical expenses need separate attention. A package price or a policy summary does not explain every exclusion, deductible or additional charge.

Planning for birth and return home

The relevant state-law parentage procedure, birth record, federal nationality and passport rules, and home-country recognition should be considered together. Establish the documents needed for each intended parent, including a parent without a genetic connection. Any proposed change of state or place of birth should be checked before the programme is altered. Allow for the practical work required after delivery rather than relying on a promised departure date.
