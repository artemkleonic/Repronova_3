Compensation following complications or death

Serious complications during donation, a surrogacy programme or related care may raise questions about treatment costs, insurance benefits and compensation. We assist with the legal assessment of these questions and, in the event of a death, can consider enquiries from family members or authorised representatives.

Identifying the possible basis for payment

We distinguish payments promised in an agreement, benefits under an insurance policy and claims for compensation based on legal responsibility for harm. These may involve different conditions, evidence and responsible parties. A complication or death does not by itself establish liability or entitlement under every arrangement.

The loss and supporting evidence

The assessment can address treatment and rehabilitation costs, loss of income and other claimed financial or non-financial harm, where relevant under the applicable framework. In a fatal case, we identify who may bring a claim or request a benefit and what authority or documentation is needed. No universal list of beneficiaries or recoverable losses is assumed.

Medical records and professional input

Work may involve organising the agreements, insurance documents, medical records and correspondence, and identifying the expert evidence needed to examine the events and their consequences. Medical causation and standards of care may require an independent clinical or forensic assessment.

Presenting and pursuing the request

Depending on the scope, we can prepare requests for information, insurance notifications or a reasoned compensation demand, and support negotiations. Litigation or locally regulated representation requires the appropriate professional arrangements. Initial contact should identify the country, the event and your relationship to the person affected; sensitive records can be requested later through an agreed channel.
