Canada changes citizenship by descent rules

Legal update · Changes effective 15 December 2025

Legal update

Changes effective 15 December 2025

Canada’s Bill C-3 came into effect on 15 December 2025. The reform changes citizenship by descent, including rules affecting families beyond the first generation born abroad. For relevant children born or adopted abroad on or after that date, a Canadian parent who was also born or adopted abroad must demonstrate a substantial connection to Canada: at least 1,095 cumulative days of physical presence before the birth or adoption.

Identify the applicable route

The relevant questions include how the parent acquired Canadian citizenship, where and when the child was born, and which provisions apply to the family. The rules for people born before the reform and those born afterwards should not be treated as interchangeable.

Prepare the documentary record

For an assisted reproduction arrangement, the citizenship analysis needs to be coordinated with advice about legal parentage and the evidence available after birth. Families should identify the documents required for their particular application and any evidence of the parent’s physical presence that may be needed.

How REPRONOVA can assist

We can help organise the legal questions and document review, coordinating Canadian advice where appropriate. A citizenship assessment should also distinguish eligibility from the separate process of obtaining proof or a passport. Programme planning should use individually verified requirements and realistic contingencies for the child’s return home.
